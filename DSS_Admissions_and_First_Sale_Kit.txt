09 Fit conversation and BANT record
BANT refers to Budget, Authority, Need and Timeline. Our questions use that framework to understand suitability. The order starts with the applicant's goal and timing, then moves to decision authority and affordability. The internal score is a DSS working method, not an externally validated predictor of conversion.
Fifteen minute conversation
Minutes zero to two: “Thank you for making time. I would like to understand what you want to improve, check the class schedule and explain the payment arrangement. We can then decide whether this cohort fits.”
Minutes two to six, need: “Tell me about a recent buyer conversation you found difficult.” Follow with: “Which part would you want to handle better?” For an aspiring seller, ask which role they are preparing for and what they have practised so far. Record the actual answer.
Minutes six to nine, timeline: “When would you want to use the skill? Can you attend both weekly sessions, including the late-December dates, and complete the exercises?” Discuss a conflict rather than assuming commitment from an enthusiastic reply.
Minutes nine to eleven, authority: “Will you approve the training yourself, or is a sponsor involved?” If another person approves, ask what they need to review and when that can happen. The learner's interest is not a sponsor's authorization.
Minutes eleven to thirteen, budget: “The tuition totals ₦80,000. Full payment and the approved ₦56,000 plus ₦24,000 arrangement are the options. Is either workable within your current plan?” Allow a clear no. Do not suggest borrowing or predict fast earnings to answer an affordability concern.
Minutes thirteen to fifteen: repeat the goal and any constraints. If suitable, provide the offer and terms, then ask whether the applicant wants to complete admission. If a key requirement does not fit, explain it and agree whether a later update would be useful.

An illustrative business with forty suitable monthly enquiries and four collected wins has 10% paid conversion. A target of six wins from the same forty enquiries would be 15% conversion and ₦180,000 gross profit, an additional ₦60,000 in that month.
That target is a scenario, not a forecast of workshop results. At the assumed unchanged margin, twelve additional completed sales would produce ₦360,000 gross profit before extra selling costs, just above a ₦350,000 workshop fee.
